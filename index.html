The short version

You do not need to finish the Solo Art Curriculum — it’s built to produce a generalist realism artist over a year or more of near-daily work. Work through Scott Robertson & Thomas Bertling’s How to Draw for spatial fundamentals, then deliberately pivot toward cartooning: Loomis, Proko, the Etherington Brothers, and Scott McCloud on comics craft.

At 1–3 hrs a week, this is a multi-year hobby, not a bootcamp. Split every session roughly 50/50 between structured practice and free drawing from day one, stay on paper through character design, and add an iPad only once you can build a character from imagination.

Key findings from the research

  • 1A clearly-taught fundamentals book beats a free but confusing one. How to Draw’s early chapters transfer to any style; its later vehicle and product-design chapters are precision-heavy repetition most cartoonists don’t need.
  • 2The Solo Art Curriculum is excellent, but built for a different artist. Its figure-drawing and character-design units are worth borrowing; its anatomy and painting sequence targets concept artists, not cartoonists.
  • 3Dedicated cartooning resources already exist and are free. Loomis, the Etherington Brothers, Proko, and McCloud cover exactly the ground a comic artist needs.
