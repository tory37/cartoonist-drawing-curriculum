The short version

Get set up with the opening of Drawabox — materials, pen grip, drawing from the shoulder. Then learn to draw from two YouTube playlists side by side: Draw Like a Sir for the concepts and Peter Han for instruction and homework. Watch a video or so of each, practice until you’re proficient (not perfect), researching your own practice material as needed, then move on. After that: construction, perspective, gesture, character design, and finally comics.

This plan is edited as it’s worked through. Modules marked done describe what actually worked; modules not started yet are the plan as it stands, and will change if something better turns up. Draw as much as you can, whenever you can — small sessions count. This is a multi-year hobby, not a bootcamp.

Principles behind the plan

  • 1The best resource is the one you’ll actually keep using. A respected textbook that feels dry loses to a well-made video series you look forward to — keep the textbook as a reference, not the main path.
  • 2Bones, not specifics. The plan names what to learn and where; finding extra drills, reference, and your own warm-up routine is part of the work.
  • 3Dedicated cartooning resources already exist and are free. Loomis, the Etherington Brothers, Proko, and McCloud cover exactly the ground a comic artist needs, once the general drawing basics are in place.
