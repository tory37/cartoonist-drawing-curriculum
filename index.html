The short version

The current phase is the big, general “learn to draw” phase, before anything specific to comics. Start with the very beginning of Drawabox (materials, pen grip, drawing from the shoulder), then work through two YouTube playlists side by side — Draw Like a Sir for the concepts and Peter Han for instruction and homework. Watch a video or so of each, practice until you’re proficient (not perfect), researching your own practice material as needed, then move on.

Everything after it is the original roadmap, kept as suggestions to consider when you get there — no plan made in advance matches how learning actually goes, so it will change.

From the original research (still just suggestions)

  • 1A clearly-taught fundamentals book beats a free but confusing one. How to Draw’s early chapters transfer to any style; its later vehicle and product-design chapters are precision-heavy repetition most cartoonists don’t need.
  • 2The Solo Art Curriculum is excellent, but built for a different artist. Its figure-drawing and character-design units are worth borrowing; its anatomy and painting sequence targets concept artists, not cartoonists.
  • 3Dedicated cartooning resources already exist and are free. Loomis, the Etherington Brothers, Proko, and McCloud cover exactly the ground a comic artist needs.
