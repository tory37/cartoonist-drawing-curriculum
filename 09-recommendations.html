Draw Your Own Comics Toolkit

09Putting it together

Start this week

  • Order How to Draw by Scott Robertson & Thomas Bertling today — it’s the very next module. Buy a fineliner pen and cheap paper while you wait for it to ship.
  • Begin Phase 1 at two short sessions a week, always splitting time with fun cartoon doodling.
  • Order Brunetti’s Cartooning: Philosophy and Practice, Barry’s Making Comics, and Mateu-Mestre’s Framed Ink now (~$70 total) — you won’t open them for a while, but shipping takes time.
  • Start Lynda Barry’s 4-minute diary this week — it needs no drawing skill and runs alongside everything else, for the whole course.

How you’ll know it’s time to move on

  • Past Phase 1–2 — your lines are noticeably more confident and a box or cylinder “sits” believably in 3D. Don’t wait for perfection.
  • Don’t chase perfect construction before moving on. Once a simple object built from a couple of overlapping shapes reads believably, move to the perspective module and start applying it loosely — precision is a means, not the goal, for a cartoonist.
  • Spend the bulk of your years in Phases 4–6 — gesture, character design, comics. This is what actually makes a cartoonist.

Adjust as you go

  • Dreading practice? Increase the play half, drop the most tedious exercise, draw more of your own characters.
  • Figures feel stiff or floaty? Double down on gesture (Phase 4) — the highest-leverage fix.
  • Already drawing confident shapes? Compress Phases 1–3 and jump toward gesture and character design faster.
