Draw Your Own Comics Toolkit

10Caveats worth remembering

  • Swapping out Drawabox is a real trade-off, not a consensus call. It’s free, thorough, and plenty of successful artists swear by it — this course dropped it as the default path because its dense, procedural writing style was a genuine dealbreaker for the person this course was built for, not because the underlying drills are bad. If Drawabox is working for you, there’s no reason to switch.
  • Free-book legality varies by title and country. Treat the Internet Archive as the safest free reading source for Loomis and Norling; buy in print if you want certainty. McCloud’s books are in copyright — use a library.
  • Some “free” resources have paid upsells — and some good resources were never free. Proko and the Solo Art Curriculum surface a lot of free content but also sell premium courses. There’s no legitimate free copy of Scott Robertson’s How to Draw — ignore any “free PDF” scan you find online; it’s still in print and in copyright. Marshall Vandruff’s perspective lectures, mentioned as an optional deep dive, are $12 — nothing in the required path costs money except the books below.
  • Links and channels change. If a specific video or playlist has moved, search the creator’s name directly — the recommendation still stands even when the URL doesn’t.
  • This course asks you to spend about $110, deliberately. Scott Robertson & Thomas Bertling’s How to Draw (~$40) replaces what used to be a fully free path through line and ellipse fundamentals — a deliberate trade for teaching quality, not a cost added lightly. Brunetti’s Cartooning: Philosophy and Practice, Barry’s Making Comics, and Mateu-Mestre’s Framed Ink add another ~$70 for the comics-craft half, chosen instead of assembling an equivalent from free YouTube links, which doesn’t really exist for that material. We looked at pricier structured alternatives too — Proko’s Marvel-branded storytelling course ($249) and Frank Santoro’s mentored correspondence course ($500) — and skipped both: good programs, but priced for someone making comics a career, and the Proko course leans mainstream-superhero rather than the loose cartoon style this course targets.
  • This is a synthesis of what free resources, a couple of inexpensive books, and their communities recommend, not a guarantee of outcomes. Progress depends almost entirely on consistent, enjoyable practice sustained over years.
