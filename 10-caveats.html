10Caveats worth remembering

  • Only using the start of Drawabox is a real trade-off, not a consensus call. It’s free, thorough, and plenty of successful artists swear by it. Its opening material (materials, pen grip, drawing from the shoulder) was genuinely useful here; the rest of the course wasn’t the right fit for the person this site is built for, which isn’t a judgment on the drills themselves. If Drawabox is working for you, there’s no reason to switch.
  • Modules you haven’t reached yet are the plan as it stands. They come from research, not experience, and real learning rarely follows a plan made in advance — expect them to change, get skipped, or get replaced once you actually get there.
  • Free-book legality varies by title and country. Treat the Internet Archive as the safest free reading source for Loomis and Norling; buy in print if you want certainty. McCloud’s books are in copyright — use a library.
  • Some “free” resources have paid upsells — and some good resources were never free. Proko and the Solo Art Curriculum surface a lot of free content but also sell premium courses. There’s no legitimate free copy of Scott Robertson’s How to Draw — ignore any “free PDF” scan you find online; it’s still in print and in copyright. Marshall Vandruff’s perspective lectures, mentioned as an optional deep dive, are $12 — nothing in the required path costs money except the books below.
  • Links and channels change. If a specific video or playlist has moved, search the creator’s name directly — the recommendation still stands even when the URL doesn’t.
  • The books are the only real cost. Scott Robertson & Thomas Bertling’s How to Draw (~$40) is recommended as a companion reference rather than required — the main drawing path is free YouTube. Brunetti’s Cartooning: Philosophy and Practice, Barry’s Making Comics, and Mateu-Mestre’s Framed Ink (~$70 together) cover the comics-craft half, chosen instead of assembling an equivalent from free YouTube links, which doesn’t really exist for that material. We looked at pricier structured alternatives too — Proko’s Marvel-branded storytelling course ($249) and Frank Santoro’s mentored correspondence course ($500) — and skipped both: good programs, but priced for someone making comics a career, and the Proko course leans mainstream-superhero rather than the loose cartoon style this course targets.
  • This is a synthesis of what free resources, a couple of inexpensive books, and their communities recommend, not a guarantee of outcomes. Progress depends almost entirely on consistent, enjoyable practice sustained over years.
