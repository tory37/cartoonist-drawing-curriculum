NOWLearning to draw

Drawabox basics, then Draw Like a Sir + Peter Han · as long as it takes

This is the big, general “learn to draw” phase — everything before getting specific about comics or cartooning. It’s the current phase of this guide; the modules after it are suggestions to consider once you get there.

Step 1 — The very beginning of Drawabox

Read the opening material of Drawabox: materials, how to hold the pen, and drawing from your shoulder instead of your wrist. That’s all you need from it here — get the setup basics and some ideas about arm movement, then move on. You don’t need to finish its lessons or do the 250 box challenge.

Step 2 — Two playlists, side by side

Draw Like a Sir

An excellent, very well produced guide to the concepts. Each video lays out what to work on, but it isn’t a homework channel — it assumes you’ll go find your own practice material for the ideas it introduces.

Peter Han’s course

More focused on direct instruction and homework: concrete exercises to practice. Lean on it for structured practice.

How to work through them

the loop Watch both playlists in order. Take the next video (or so) from each, then stop and practice what it covered. Don’t move on until you have some real proficiency with it — not mastery, just the feeling that you can do it on purpose. That might take one week, two weeks, or longer. Then watch the next video in each and repeat.
  • Proficiency, not mastery. You’ll keep coming back to every one of these ideas for years. The bar for moving on is “I get it and can do it reasonably,” not “it’s perfect.”
  • Solo research is the key skill here. Neither playlist hands you everything you need to practice, Draw Like a Sir especially. When a concept doesn’t click, or you run out of things to draw, go look it up: other tutorials on the same topic, drills and worksheets, reference photos, other artists’ takes. Finding your own practice material is part of the work.
  • Mix drills and play. Drill the concept, then use it in drawings you actually want to make.
  • Save drills that work. When you find an exercise worth coming back to, add it to the toolkit so it’s easy to warm up with later.

When this phase is done

Once you’ve worked through both playlists, look over the suggested modules that follow and pick what fits. They’re a starting point, not a fixed path, and this guide will be updated as things change.
