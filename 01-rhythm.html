Draw Your Own Comics Toolkit

01Weekly rhythm & supplies

Progress here comes from consistency, not intensity. Structure it like this:

  • Two sessions of 45–90 minutes a week (or one longer weekend block plus small doodling on the side).
  • Split every session roughly 50/50 — structured exercise from your current phase, then free “play”: doodle your own characters, copy cartoons you love, sketch from shows.
  • Don’t chase finished pieces early. The reps are the point, not the polish.
  • Expect each phase to take months. That’s normal, and it’s what makes this sustainable for five years instead of five weeks.

Supplies — keep it cheap

✎ HB pencil + a softer 2B/3B
✎ Vinyl or kneaded eraser
✎ Canson XL or Strathmore sketchbook
✎ Staedtler Mars Lumograph pencils
✎ A pack of fineliners (for ink line drills — no erasing)
✎ Printer paper is fine to start

Four books worth buying (~$110 total, one-time)

Everything else in this course is free, but four real, proven, expert-designed texts are worth paying for instead of us improvising an equivalent from YouTube:

📖 Scott Robertson & Thomas Bertling, How to Draw (~$40)
📖 Ivan Brunetti, Cartooning: Philosophy and Practice (~$20)
📖 Lynda Barry, Making Comics (~$25)
📖 Marcos Mateu-Mestre, Framed Ink (~$25)

Order How to Draw first — it’s the very next module. Brunetti’s escalating drills, Barry’s teaching approach, and Mateu-Mestre’s panel-staging system don’t come into play until the comics module, but all four are real, classroom-tested courses in book form, worth ordering now while shipping catches up.

Start this now: the 4-minute diary

Before any fundamentals, start a habit you’ll keep for the whole course — Lynda Barry’s 4-minute diary. Split a page into four boxes: 7 things you did (2 min), 7 things you saw (2 min), one thing you overheard (30 sec), and one quick sketch from the day (30 sec).

why it matters It takes zero drawing skill to start, and it builds the observation and finishing habits that storytelling actually depends on — running in parallel with the hand-control drills in the next module, not after them. Do it a few times a week from here on, for the whole course.
