The start of Drawabox · a week or two

The very beginning of Drawabox

Read the opening material of Drawabox: materials, how to hold the pen, and drawing from your shoulder instead of your wrist. That’s all you need from it — the setup basics and some ideas about arm movement. You don’t need to go further into its lessons or do the 250 box challenge.

Weekly rhythm

Progress comes from consistency, not intensity. Structure it like this:

  • Two sessions of 45–90 minutes a week (or one longer weekend block plus small doodling on the side).
  • Split every session roughly 50/50 — structured practice from your current module, then free “play”: doodle your own characters, copy cartoons you love, sketch from shows.
  • Don’t chase finished pieces early. The reps are the point, not the polish.
  • Expect each module to take months. That’s normal, and it’s what makes this sustainable for five years instead of five weeks.

Supplies — keep it cheap

✎ HB pencil + a softer 2B/3B
✎ Vinyl or kneaded eraser
✎ Canson XL or Strathmore sketchbook
✎ Staedtler Mars Lumograph pencils
✎ A pack of fineliners (for ink line drills — no erasing)
✎ Printer paper is fine to start

Books worth having

📖 Scott Robertson & Thomas Bertling, How to Draw (~$40)
📖 Ivan Brunetti, Cartooning: Philosophy and Practice (~$20)
📖 Lynda Barry, Making Comics (~$25)
📖 Marcos Mateu-Mestre, Framed Ink (~$25)

How to Draw is a respected perspective and form textbook — Robertson and Bertling both taught for years at Art Center College of Design. It’s recommended, but as a reference to dip into, not the main path: it gets dry fast for a beginner, and the playlists in the next module turned out to be a better way in. Brunetti, Barry, and Mateu-Mestre don’t come into play until the comics module.

Worth considering: the 4-minute diary

Lynda Barry’s 4-minute diary: split a page into four boxes — 7 things you did (2 min), 7 things you saw (2 min), one thing you overheard (30 sec), and one quick sketch from the day (30 sec).

why it’s here It takes zero drawing skill, and it builds the observation and finishing habits that storytelling depends on. It isn’t required to move on — keep it on the list and pick it up whenever it fits, a few times a week.
