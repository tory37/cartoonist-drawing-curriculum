Draw Your Own Comics Toolkit

05Gesture & simplified anatomy

Proko, Love Life Drawing, Loomis’ Fun With a Pencil · roughly 8–12 weeks, then ongoing

This is the pivot away from pure fundamentals drilling and toward cartooning proper.

  • Proko — free gesture and figure-drawing videos on YouTube. Gesture is the single most important skill for expressive cartooning; it’s what keeps drawings from looking stiff.
  • Love Life Drawing — beginner-friendly figure/gesture on YouTube.
  • Line of Action / Quickposes — free timed-reference websites. Note: both include nude reference photos by default alongside clothed ones — both let you filter this in their settings.
  • Andrew Loomis, Fun With a Pencil — free and legal on the Internet Archive. The first ~30 pages teach cartoon heads and figures via ball-and-plane construction, the perfect bridge from fundamentals to cartoon character.

Make it a weekly habit, not a single session

Gesture doesn’t come from one good session — it comes from a repeated cycle, which is exactly how it was actually taught. For twenty years, Disney animator Walt Stanchfield ran weekly gesture classes for the studio’s own animators (his students include Glen Keane, Brad Bird, and John Lasseter): draw from a model, get critiqued, repeat, week after week. His own class handouts are freely shared online with his family’s blessing at Thinking Animation — read a few alongside your own weekly sessions; they’re as much about attitude as technique.

how to practice Short timed gestures (30 seconds–2 minutes), then “mannequinize” into simple shapes. Chase flow and exaggeration, not accuracy — and keep it weekly for the whole course, not just this phase.
skip / deprioritize Rigorous écorché/muscle anatomy, “100 heads / 100 hands” realism challenges, and the Solo Art Curriculum’s multi-term anatomy sequence. You need enough anatomy to caricature it, not medical accuracy.
